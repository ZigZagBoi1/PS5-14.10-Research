## Shared unimplemented-handler behavior

Supplied sysent metadata identifies 631 as a zero-argument entry sharing `nosys`; body `0xffffffff8af220e0`–`0xffffffff8af22187` corroborates a final constant return 78. It is not a bare constant-return stub: it obtains parent pointer at caller `+0x08`, calls `0xffffffff8ae86a00(parent+0x148,0,0,0)`, invokes `0xffffffff8ac18ff0(local,0x70)` (zeroing inferred), places u32 12 at local `+0x10` and u32 `0x10006` at local `+0x18`, calls `0xffffffff8af20540(parent,caller,12,local)`, then calls `0xffffffff8ae86fa0(parent+0x148,0,0,0)`.

The paired helpers are lock/unlock-like by consistent argument shape, but their bodies and the record-consuming helper are not supplied. The record may convey an event/signal-like notification; assigning a concrete event or known function name would exceed the evidence. No argument block is read, no selector dispatch or explicit permission gate is visible, and no user pointer or variable-length copy occurs. Record construction and the unreviewed helper are the limited remaining surface. The original offset-zero trampoline is shared and must not be mistaken for an independent implementation of syscall 631. Stack-guard mismatch calls `0xffffffff8b0bd6b0` then UD2.
