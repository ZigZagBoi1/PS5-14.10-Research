=== CRITICAL ANALYSIS: B ≠ T - 0x60 ===

v4c (zero metadata) and v4d (valid metadata) BOTH crash at the same redirect
target (T-0x20). The metadata content is irrelevant to the crash — the issue
is that T-0x20 is NOT within our spray buffer.

The formula B = T - 0x60 from P588b applies to the **80-byte size class**:
  - In P588b (route d, null m_clients path):
    - FontFace wrapper (72B, class 80) reclaimed the freed 80B table slot at B
    - New table allocated at B+0x50 (next contiguous 80B block)
    - T (bucket start) = B+0x50 + 0x10 (metadata prefix) = B+0x60
  - So B = T - 0x60 gives the wrapper block in the 80B class

Our spray buffers are 192B ArrayBuffers, allocated in a DIFFERENT size class.
They occupy a different heap region from the 80B table allocation. The address
T is in the 80B region; our buffer is in the 192B region. T-0x20 points
somewhere in the 80B region (probably inside the FontFace wrapper or preceding
block), NOT into our 192B spray buffer.

PROOF: T varies across runs (ASLR), but the crash is consistent regardless of
T's specific value. If T-0x20 were inside our buffer (which we control), the
metadata would differ between v4c (zeros) and v4d (valid), producing different
behavior. Instead, both crash identically — the address is unmapped or contains
fatal values that are INDEPENDENT of what we write to our buffer.

=== IMPLICATIONS ===

1. We CANNOT compute the absolute address of our 192B spray buffer from T.
   T is in the 80B heap region; our buffer is in the 192B region. The two
   regions have no fixed relationship.

2. To forge a hash table that removeClient reads, we need EITHER:
   a) The spray buffer's absolute VA (unknown, no oracle)
   b) A writable region whose address we DO know (the only known one is the
      80B region near T, but we can't write to it from JS)
   c) A different exploitation strategy that doesn't require forging the table

3. The GC destructor path is CONFIRMED and the deref at +0x00 is a reliable
   write primitive (rc decrement). The removeClient read at +0x58 is confirmed.
   The missing piece is WHERE to point +0x58.
